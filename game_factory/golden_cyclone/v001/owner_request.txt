# PART 1 — PERMANENT GRAPHICS FACTORY RULES

## RULE 001 — ONE FRAME PER IMAGE

Every production animation frame MUST be created as one independent image-generation output.

Absolutely forbidden:

- Generating 6 poses inside one image
- Generating 12 poses inside one image
- Using a collage as the source of production frames
- Cropping tiny characters from a storyboard and calling them full-quality sprites
- Dividing a 1024x1024 collage into low-resolution character frames
- Upscaling those small cropped sprites to pretend they are high-resolution assets

Required:

ONE FRAME = ONE GENERATION REQUEST = ONE PNG FILE.

Example:

hero_battle_golden_cyclone_001.png
hero_battle_golden_cyclone_002.png
hero_battle_golden_cyclone_003.png
hero_battle_golden_cyclone_004.png

Each image must contain exactly ONE complete character in ONE specific animation pose.

No additional character.

No other poses.

No labels.

No borders.

No storyboard panels.

No sprite-sheet layouts during the generation stage.

## RULE 002 — FULL-QUALITY NATIVE SOURCE

For battle characters, use a large, detailed source image.

Preferred source canvas:
1024x1024 pixels or higher when supported.

The original character should occupy most of the useful canvas while leaving enough room for movement.

Do not sacrifice character detail to fit multiple poses into one image.

All frames must preserve:

- Face details
- Hair strands and silhouette
- Feather structure
- Costume patterns
- Gold ornaments
- Rope belt
- Leather details
- Consistent anatomy
- Original color palette

Keep the original high-resolution PNG.

If Godot requires smaller native pixel-art sprites, create a separately validated game-resolution derivative from each original frame.

Do not assume that downscaling automatically produces high-quality pixel art.

Review each derivative at its actual displayed size.

Never overwrite the high-resolution master.

## RULE 003 — TRANSPARENT BACKGROUND

All character body frames must have genuine transparency.

Required:
RGBA PNG.

No:
- black painted background
- white background
- checkerboard background
- battlefield background
- floor
- environment
- baked character shadow

Keep character shadows as separate assets.

## RULE 004 — NO BUILT-IN WEAPONS

All character body animation frames must remain weaponless.

The Hero can pose as though gripping a sword, but the sword itself must not be rendered into the character body PNG.

Do not include:

- Blade
- Hilt
- Guard
- Pommel
- Permanent weapon
- Weapon trails baked into body sprites

Weapons come from Weapon Factory.

Separate:
- Hero Body
- Weapon
- Weapon Trail
- Attack VFX
- Ground Effects
- Shadow

Use per-frame weapon attachment anchors.

Support changing the sword without regenerating Hero's body.

## RULE 005 — LOCKED HERO DESIGN AND COLORS

Use these approved references:

PRIMARY STYLE REFERENCE:
hero_battle_idle_ready_01__attempt_01.png

SECONDARY REFERENCE:
hero_battle_step_01__attempt_01.png

Preserve their character identity, rendering quality, proportions, and color balance.

Pay special attention to:
- Brown hair
- Natural skin color
- Blue scarf
- Controlled blue feathers
- Controlled red accents
- White and cream feathers
- Brown pants
- Gold ornaments
- Leather bindings
- Shell accessories

Do not make every new attack pose more saturated blue or red.

Do not randomly alter costume details between frames.

Use the approved Hero battle palette.

The Hero must look like the SAME person in every frame.

## RULE 006 — ONE FILE PER GRAPHICAL ELEMENT

This applies to EVERY graphics request in skl-SF.

Examples:

Characters:
One animation frame per PNG.

Weapons:
One weapon asset per PNG.

Effects:
One VFX frame per PNG.

Environment:
One floor tile or individual modular asset per PNG.

Architecture:
One independently reusable architectural piece per PNG.

Furniture:
One furniture asset and direction per PNG.

Decorations:
One isolated decorative object per PNG.

UI:
One independent icon or graphical component per PNG.

Do not merge unrelated production assets into one image.

Exceptions are permitted only for explicitly approved final flattened artwork, never for reusable source assets.

## RULE 007 — SPRITE SHEETS ARE EXPORTS ONLY

The production workflow must be:

1. Generate individual high-quality PNGs.
2. Validate each PNG.
3. Align individual frame canvases.
4. Create animation metadata.
5. Assemble Sprite Sheets automatically.
6. Create GIF or video previews.
7. Import frames or generated atlases into Godot.

Sprite Sheets must be generated FROM the independent PNG frames.

Never produce the individual production PNGs by cropping a low-resolution generated sprite sheet.

Keep the original PNGs permanently.

## RULE 008 — PRESERVE APPROVED GRAPHICS

Never overwrite approved assets.

When improving an existing frame, create a new version.

Example:

hero_battle_attack_001_v2.png

Keep the previous version available.

Update the active asset manifest only after validation and approval.

Never claim generated images exist unless the actual PNG files have been verified.

PHASE A — ANTICIPATION

Frames 001–004

- Hero lowers his center of gravity.
- His shoulders begin rotating.
- Feet adjust into a stable fighting stance.
- His gripping hand prepares for the spin.
- Feathers and cloth begin following the movement.

PHASE B — ROTATION BUILDUP

Frames 005–008

- Hero rotates his hips and shoulders.
- His footwork visibly drives the rotation.
- His clothing and feathers lag naturally behind.
- His hand follows an arc around his body.
- The pose becomes more dynamic.

PHASE C — FULL SPIN

Frames 009–014

- Hero performs a controlled full-body rotation.
- Use genuine different body orientations.
- Show rear, side, and front-facing transitional views as needed.
- Do not mirror a single image to simulate a 360-degree spin.
- Maintain consistent anatomy and costume design.
- The rotation must make physical sense.

PHASE D — POWERFUL STRIKE

Frames 015–018

- Hero completes the spin.
- Weapon-holding hand accelerates toward the opponent.
- Torso and hips transfer momentum.
- The final strike has strong visual impact.
- Golden strike effects are handled separately.

PHASE E — RECOVERY

Frames 019–022

- Hero slows down.
- Feet regain stable contact.
- Cloth and feathers settle naturally.
- Hero returns smoothly to battle idle position.

# PART 8 — ANIMATION QUALITY CONTROL

After creating each image, validate:

1. Exactly one character is present.
2. Character is complete and not cropped.
3. Transparent background is genuine.
4. Image resolution meets source-quality requirements.
5. Hair and face match the approved references.
6. Clothing patterns are consistent.
7. Blue and red saturation remain consistent.
8. Hands and feet are anatomically correct.
9. No equipped weapon is baked in.
10. The frame differs meaningfully from neighboring frames.
11. The pose matches its animation phase.
12. Ground anchor is consistent.
13. No unexpected costume redesign.
14. File is stored in the correct folder.

Automate checks for:
- PNG validity
- Dimensions
- Alpha channel
- Filename
- Duplicate frames
- Manifest completeness
- Animation frame order

Use visual review for identity, artistic quality, and animation coherence.

Do not rely only on automatic tests to claim visual quality.
